Before departure

Weather & packing

Early April in Korea

Expect spring weather, but mornings and evenings can still feel cool, especially outside Seoul or if staying late after the barbecue after-party.

Clothing

Layers, light jacket, comfortable walking shoes, wedding outfit, and warmer layer for evening.

Useful items

Umbrella, travel adapter if needed, medication, passport copy, and offline copies of key addresses.

Wedding note

The ceremony is traditional and the venue has a countryside feel, so comfort and respectful clothing matter.

Cards and cash

Cards are widely used in Korea, but it is still useful to keep some cash for markets, transport-card top-ups, small shops, and emergencies.

WOWPASS

WOWPASS is a tourist-friendly prepaid card and app for Korea. It can be used for cashless payments, foreign-currency top-ups, KRW withdrawal at machines, and public transport through its Tmoney function.

Open WOWPASS official site

Simple recommendation

For most guests, a normal international card plus either WOWPASS or a Tmoney card should be enough. WOWPASS is convenient if you want one card for payments and public transport.

Transport

Getting around Korea

Recommended setup

Use WOWPASS if you want a tourist card that combines payments and public transport, or use a standard Tmoney card if you only need subway and bus access.

WOWPASS official site

Tmoney basics

Tmoney is the classic transport card for buses and subways. Tap when entering and exiting subway gates, and tap when boarding and getting off buses.

How Tmoney works on public transport

Station exits

Korean subway stations can be large. Follow the exit number shown in your map app.

Taxis

For the wedding venue and countryside areas, taxi or pre-arranged local travel may be much easier than relying on buses.

Culture

Korean manners & etiquette

Small gestures help guests feel respectful without overthinking every situation.

Greetings

A light bow or handshake is fine. The stereotypical “praying hands” greeting is not Korean and can feel offensive, so please avoid it.

Two hands

Giving or receiving something with two hands feels polite, especially with older people.

Shoes

Remove shoes in homes and some traditional indoor spaces.

Public transport

Keep voices low, queue, and avoid phone calls on trains or buses.

Photos

Ask before taking close photos of people. At the wedding, follow any announcement from the families or venue.

Trash

Public bins can be limited. Keep small trash with you until you find a bin.
