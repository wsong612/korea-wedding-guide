Seoul, by mood

Neighbourhood guide

Use these as starting points when choosing accommodation and planning free days before or after the wedding.

Ikseon-dong

Small hanok alleys, cafés, and an atmospheric introduction to older Seoul.

Seongsu

Design shops, converted industrial spaces, coffee, and a modern local feel.

Bukchon

Hanok streets and views near the palaces. Best visited respectfully and not too late.

Hongdae / Yeonnam

Good for younger guests, casual restaurants, music, cafés, and nightlife.

Markets

Gwangjang, Namdaemun, Mangwon, and Myeongdong street food.

Views & nature

Namsan, Han River parks, Seoul Forest, and Bukhansan for active guests.

Don’t miss

Classic Seoul sightseeing

Gyeongbokgung Palace

Seoul’s grandest royal palace. Go early, especially if you want to rent hanbok and take photos before it becomes crowded.

View on map →

Changdeokgung + Insadong

A quieter palace atmosphere combined with tea houses, galleries, crafts, and traditional streets.

Beyond Seoul

Other places to consider

Optional ideas for guests who want to extend the trip beyond Seoul and the wedding area.

Gyeongju

History, temples, royal tombs, and a strong traditional atmosphere.

Busan

Sea, markets, cafés, seafood, and a relaxed city atmosphere.

Jeonju

Hanok village, food, and a slower traditional city feel.

Sokcho / Seoraksan

Mountains, nature, and hiking.

Korean food

Colourful food photos belong here

Food photos do not need to match the palette. Let the food be colourful and use the website colours around the photos.

A meal

Bibimbap, soups, rice, banchan, and home-style dishes.

BBQ

A social meal and good fit for groups.

Street food

Tteokbokki, hotteok, kimbap, skewers, and market snacks.

Noodles

Kalguksu, naengmyeon, ramyeon, and other comforting options.

Drinks

Soju, beer, makgeolli, cafés, and non-alcoholic drinks.
